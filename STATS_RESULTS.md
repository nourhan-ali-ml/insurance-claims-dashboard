# Statistical Analysis — Hypothesis Testing & Regression

Reproduce with `python stats_analysis.py` (needs `fact_claims.csv` + `dim_customers.csv`).
Charts and raw numbers are in `stats_output/`.

## Method choices (and why)

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| Cost = `billed_amount` | Defined for every claim. `paid_amount` is 0 for Denied/Pending claims and would distort a cost comparison. |
| Non-parametric tests + log scale | Claim cost is strongly right-skewed (skew = 5.15), so a plain t-test on raw cost is not appropriate. |
| Hypothesis tests at **customer level** | Claims are clustered within customers (ICC ≈ 0.51). Treating 162K correlated rows as independent understates standard errors (naive SE was ~22% too small vs customer-level). |
| Regression at claim level with **customer-clustered SEs** | Keeps claim-level cost as the outcome while correcting for within-customer correlation. |

## 1. Smokers vs non-smokers (Current vs Never)

Unit: average claim cost per customer (7,593 current smokers vs 43,387 never-smokers).

| Metric | Current | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | $1,484 | $963 |
| Median | $1,012 | $635 |

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Mann-Whitney U | p < 0.001; P(smoker cost > non-smoker cost) = 0.64; rank-biserial r = 0.28 (small-to-medium) |
| Welch t-test on log cost | geometric-mean ratio = **1.57** (95% CI 1.54 – 1.60), p < 0.001 |
| Mean difference | **+$521** (95% bootstrap CI $485 – $557) |
| Median difference | **+$377** (95% bootstrap CI $349 – $401) |
| Kruskal-Wallis, Never / Former / Current | H = 1,563, p < 0.001 |

Interpretation: current smokers' claims cost roughly 57% more, and the whole confidence interval is far from zero. The effect is real but moderate in rank terms (a random smoker's claim is costlier than a random non-smoker's about 64% of the time, not 99%).

## 2. Regression on claim cost

Model: `log(billed_amount) ~ age + bmi + smoker + plan_type` (reference: Never-smoker, PPO), n = 162,178, customer-clustered SEs.

| Term | % change in cost | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current smoker | **+59.1%** | 54.7 – 63.7 | < 0.001 |
| Former smoker | **+15.6%** | 12.8 – 18.5 | < 0.001 |
| Age (per year) | +0.56% | 0.50 – 0.62 | < 0.001 |
| BMI (per unit) | +0.99% | 0.80 – 1.18 | < 0.001 |
| Plan: EPO vs PPO | −1.1% | −3.9 – +1.7 | 0.43 |
| Plan: HMO vs PPO | +0.4% | −1.8 – +2.6 | 0.73 |
| Plan: POS vs PPO | +1.6% | −1.3 – +4.6 | 0.28 |

* R² = 0.026. The model explains little of the claim-to-claim variation (expected: individual claim cost is noisy). Treat coefficients as average effects, not as a prediction tool.
* VIF for age and BMI ≈ 1.0 (no multicollinearity).
* Robustness: a Gamma GLM with log link on raw cost gives the same picture (Current smoker +56.8%, Former +14.8%, age +0.57%/yr, BMI +0.89%/unit, plans ≈ 0).

## 3. Comparing plans (PPO / HMO / EPO / POS)

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Kruskal-Wallis | H = 7.82, p = 0.050 |
| ANOVA on log cost (confirmation) | F = 2.71, p = 0.043 |
| Effect size ε² | 0.00008 (negligible) |
| Dunn post-hoc, Holm-adjusted | Only EPO vs POS reaches p = 0.036; all other pairs p > 0.2 |

Mean claim cost per customer: PPO $1,052 · HMO $1,056 · EPO $1,029 · POS $1,074.

Interpretation: the omnibus test sits right at the 0.05 line and the effect size is essentially zero, and the regression also finds no plan effect after adjusting for smoking, age and BMI. Conclusion: **claim cost does not differ meaningfully by plan type.** Cost trends are driven by smoking status, age and BMI, not by plan.

## Limitations (say these out loud in an interview)

1. **Claim-level amounts are synthetic.** The original data is customer-level; individual claim amounts were generated by splitting each customer's `total_claims_paid`. Differences between groups therefore come from the customer-level totals in the source data, and within-customer variation is simulated noise. This analysis demonstrates the method, not real-world causal findings.
2. **Observational data.** Smoking is associated with higher cost, but this does not prove causation (confounders such as chronic conditions are not in the model).
3. **Large n makes tiny effects "significant".** That is why effect sizes and confidence intervals are reported next to every p-value, and why the plan result (p ≈ 0.05, ε² ≈ 0) is read as "no practical difference".
